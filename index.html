Naruto D&D-style RPG

by RED7734

Naruto D&D-style RPG is a D&D-style experience set in the Naruto universe. Its goal is to bring the feel of a D&D campaign into the shinobi world, while removing mechanics like Hit Points and Armor Class, rewarding imagination over pure combat math.

WARNING: this is NOT classic D&D. The name may be misleading, so read this well: this is a D&D-style RPG, not D&D. It is inspired by D&D, but it is its own thing, with its own rules.

Here, narrative matters more than numbers. There are no Hit Points and no Armor Class to whittle down or beat. You describe what your character does, roll the skill that fits, and the DM decides what happens from your roll and your description: how well a strike lands, how badly someone is hurt, what a technique really does. Your imagination and the story you are telling count for more than the math, so the DM's judgement is the center of every fight.

Character Creation

  • 27 points to assign across Ability Scores (each starts at a base of 8). Every 4 levels, 2 more points are gained.
  • Chakra: 5 points per level by default, adjustable per character in the Chakra section (e.g. 15/level for an Uzumaki).
  • Leveling uses a Milestone system, the DM decides when the party levels up based on the story, not experience points.

Combat

Roll Taijutsu, Ninjutsu, Genjutsu, Fūinjutsu, Shurikenjutsu, or Weapons, depending on what you want to use. If the technique costs chakra, spend the corresponding Chakra Points. Whether the attack lands and how much impact it has is decided by the DM, based on:

  • the number rolled
  • how the player describes using the jutsu (creativity, coherence, narrative effectiveness)
  • whether what's described is plausible and feasible in the DM's judgment

There are no Hit Points. Combat ends when the DM determines a winner, not when someone hits zero. The system rewards whoever best uses imagination to describe and execute interesting, effective attacks.

Shurikenjutsu vs Weapons

Shurikenjutsu covers thrown weapons: throwing a shuriken, a kunai, senbon, or any similar projectile. Weapons covers everything wielded in hand or used up close: a kunai used as a knife, a sword, a tantō, nunchaku, a puppet, or even a bow.

Dexterity vs Strength

Taijutsu can be rolled with either Dexterity or Strength, because they represent two completely different martial philosophies, and both are equally valid ways to build a taijutsu-focused character.

Dexterity-based Taijutsu is the taijutsu of precision, speed, technique, and control over movement. The character isn't necessarily trying to hit as hard as possible, they're trying to make sure the strike lands exactly where it needs to, ideally before the opponent can even react. Think Neji Hyūga: fast, fluid movement, dodges and repositioning, extremely precise strikes, clever use of angles and distance, hits aimed at specific points on the body, and the ability to chain many strikes together quickly, all with less reliance on raw physical power. The idea is: "I don't need to be stronger than you, I need to be fast and precise enough to hit you before you can stop me." The Gentle Fist is the perfect example, Neji doesn't need to break through an opponent's body, precise chakra-guided strikes are enough to damage their chakra circulatory system. In D&D terms, Dexterity represents accuracy, speed, finesse, and footwork.

Strength-based Taijutsu flips that principle. The fighter uses their own body as a heavy weapon. The movements can still be technical and fast, but the end result depends above all on how much physical force they can generate and transfer. Think Rock Lee: devastating punches and kicks, serious muscular power, huge physical impact, the ability to smash through defenses, mostly direct attacks, and techniques that further amplify raw physical power. The idea is: "You can know exactly where I'm about to hit. You still have to be tough enough to survive it."

Chakra Control

Chakra Control is the skill of shaping and directing your own chakra with precision. It's the ability behind walking up walls or trees, walking on water, and any similar feat of controlling chakra flow through the body. It also governs techniques that are formed and shaped directly from raw chakra rather than a specific nature transformation: the Rasengan, for example, requires Chakra Control because it's simply kneaded chakra spun into a dense ball in the palm, with no elemental nature involved. In the Jutsu Library, Rasengan is rolled with Chakra Control for this reason, not Ninjutsu.

Chakra Control is also one of the best tools a player has for inventing new techniques on the fly. A skilled shinobi can reshape an existing jutsu into something new by controlling how the chakra is expressed. As an example, in a personal campaign, a player using an Uchiha character took the Great Fireball Technique and, through Chakra Control, reshaped it into a continuous stream of fire from the mouth, functioning like a flamethrower, effectively creating a brand new technique out of an existing one. Chakra Control is therefore one of the most important and versatile skills a shinobi can have, and how far it can be pushed is limited mostly by imagination.

Chakra Recovery

Chakra is restored by sleeping, by the passage of time, or through narrative remedies (ninja pills, a Medical-Nin's jutsu, etc). There are no fixed rules here, it's up to the DM and players to decide together in-game what works and how much chakra is recovered, case by case. After a fight, it's often necessary to rest or sleep to recover chakra, if the DM allows it. A Medical-Nin in the party might know a jutsu to restore chakra faster. There are many options, just use your imagination.

Constitution represents endurance on both fronts, physical and spiritual. Roll it whenever it's relevant to check how well the character recovers, physically from wounds and exhaustion, spiritually in terms of chakra. It also comes into play when judging how well a character can conserve or push their chakra reserves in the middle of a fight, or how quickly they bounce back afterward.

Chakra Consumption

Some techniques don't just have a flat chakra cost, they scale with how much you use them. The Multiple Shadow Clone Technique, for example, costs chakra per clone: if the technique is set at 10 chakra, then every single clone made costs another 10 chakra, so making three clones costs 30. That kind of drain adds up fast, which is why the technique is usually best suited to shinobi with unusually large chakra reserves, like a Jinchūriki or a member of the Uzumaki clan.

Chakra: Every character receives 5 Chakra Points per level as the standard, suggested amount built into the character creator. This is the default baseline and the recommended playstyle for most campaigns.

This default is not a hard restriction. The Chakra section now has its own Chakra per Level field, set to 5 by default, that you can freely change to fit your character or story. Raise it to 15 for an Uzumaki to represent the clan's naturally enormous reserves, give a Jinchūriki or another exceptionally powerful shinobi an even larger pool, or lower it for a chakra-poor character, whatever fits your playstyle.

In short: 5 Chakra Points per level is the suggested default, not an absolute rule. Change the rate to match your character concept, your campaign, and your table. Your game, your rules.

More generally, every chakra cost shown in the Jutsu Library is just a suggested example. Nothing stops a player or DM from freely rebalancing how much chakra any technique costs to better fit their own campaign's pace and power level. Example: Susanoo costs 100 Chakra because it's an extremely powerful technique meant to require a high level to use, but if you still find it too broken, raise it to 200. Or maybe you want a less punitive campaign where players get to enjoy strong jutsu more often, then lower it. Your game, your choices, as long as every player and the DM at your table agree on it.

Alternative Ruleset: Chakra as a Threshold

If you find managing chakra too tiring and mechanical, there is an alternative ruleset where your chakra level is only the number you need to reach to perform a technique. It replaces spending and tracking chakra with storytelling and the DM's judgement.

How it works: a technique's chakra cost becomes a requirement. If a technique costs 50 chakra, your character must have at least 50 chakra in their body to perform it, so your Chakra Points must read 50/50 or more. Once you reach that number, you can use the technique freely, and even several techniques, without clicking Cast Jutsu, so you don't spend chakra and never have to keep managing it.

The DM's role: because nothing is spent, the DM is the one who tells you when you can't use a technique again, for example: "no, you can't reuse this technique, you used it a moment ago and you have used too much chakra". The DM can be more lenient with cheaper techniques. If your reserve is much bigger than the cost, for example 100 chakra for a 50 chakra technique, the DM may let you use it for longer or more times. If you only have 50 chakra and you use that technique once, you are dry, but you may still be able to use a few small jutsu that would only take about 5 chakra. Think of it as a story: how big the technique is compared to your reserve, how long the fight has lasted, and how tired your character would realistically be.

Choosing it: this is an alternative, not a replacement. Spending and tracking chakra remains the default, and the whole table, DM included, should agree on using this one. It also works with the Chakra per Level field: a bigger reserve means you can reach the number for more techniques, and gives the DM more room to be generous.

Chakra Knowledge

Chakra Knowledge is the theoretical side of chakra: not shaping or spending it yourself, like Chakra Control does, but understanding it. It covers how jutsu are built and how they work under the hood, how jinchūriki and tailed beasts function, how forbidden jutsu came to exist, and chakra-related phenomena in general.

In practice, roll Chakra Knowledge whenever a character is trying to understand a technique rather than perform one: studying a jutsu they've witnessed well enough to copy it (this replaces using Chakra Control for that purpose), reverse-engineering how an unfamiliar or forbidden technique functions, or researching and developing a brand-new jutsu of their own from first principles. It's also the right roll for characters built around research rather than combat, an Orochimaru-style or Kabuto-style character piecing together a new forbidden jutsu, studying an existing one they've gotten their hands on, or investigating something like a jinchūriki's seal or a Kekkei Genkai they don't personally have, are all Chakra Knowledge in action.

Fūinjutsu

Fūinjutsu usage

Fūinjutsu isn't limited to casting sealing jutsu in combat. It's a broadly creative skill that can be used to imprint a jutsu onto a scroll for later use, or to imprint a seal onto a scroll that, once opened, summons an item or even a person. This is also a great way to manage inventory space if you and your DM are playing with realistic carry limits: a tent kit, a cooking kit, or anything else that would otherwise eat up a lot of inventory space can be compressed into a seal on a scroll by rolling Fūinjutsu with an empty scroll. Later, breaking the seal releases the stored object. There are many clever ways to use Fūinjutsu beyond direct combat, limited only by your imagination.

A filled scroll can also hold a coded message instead of an object or jutsu. In that case, Fūinjutsu is rolled to open or otherwise use the seal itself, while Investigation is rolled separately to actually decipher what the message says.

Summoning Jutsu & Animal bond

To use a Summoning Jutsu, first find an animal or group of animals with which your ninja can establish a bond. This can be a giant, intelligent summon such as a giant frog, slug, or snake capable of speaking or meaningfully relating to the character, or an ordinary realistic animal such as a real snake or crow. It can even be a group of animals, such as many dogs, many crows, or many sharks. There is no requirement that the bond be with a single giant summon.

Once the animal or animals have been found, roll Animal Handling during roleplay to see how successfully the ninja relates to them. The dice result determines the outcome of that bonding attempt. If the bonding succeeds, establish the relationship in roleplay. Then take an Empty Scroll and use Fūinjutsu to write the summoning seal for that animal or group, and finally sign the contract with your blood.

When the contract is successfully completed, remove the Empty Scroll from the Inventory and add a Summoning Scroll. This represents the blank scroll having been successfully turned into a completed summoning contract. The Summoning Scroll then works passively from your Inventory whenever you perform the corresponding Summoning Jutsu.

You can then add Summoning Technique (Kuchiyose no Jutsu) to your Jutsu Library, but establish through roleplay how your character learned it, whether from a master, another shinobi, the summon itself, or another appropriate source. When you perform the jutsu, roll Ninjutsu. The Summoning Scroll containing your blood contract acts passively and determines which specific animal or group of animals you summon. You do not need to roll Fūinjutsu every time you summon once the contract has already been successfully created.

After the summon appears, you can command it with Animal Handling. The higher the roll, the better your control and coordination with the animal or group, and the better you command them, the more effectively they can fight alongside you.

Use the Companion Animals section (below Weapons, above Inventory) to keep track of any animal or group your character is bonded to, whether or not it's tied to a Summoning Jutsu contract: a single giant summon, an ordinary animal, a whole pack, a ninken (a ninja dog), a Nara's bonded deer, or an Aburame's kikaichū colony. Ninken aren't exclusive to the Inuzuka (Kakashi Hatake famously has his own), but an Inuzuka's ninken is raised alongside them since childhood, growing up together rather than being trained separately. Each entry has a name, a type (Ninken, Insect Colony, Summoning Animal/Group, or Other Bonded Animal), and a free-text description, and can be rolled against Animal Handling directly from that section. Note that a ninken, a bonded deer, or an insect colony isn't summoned the way a Summoning Jutsu bond is, it's tracked in the same section for convenience regardless. An Inuzuka character can also pick a personal ninken straight from the Ninken Partner feature in their Clan section, and a Nara character can pick a bonded deer from the Bonded Deer feature; hitting Apply Clan Traits adds the chosen (or custom-named) companion directly to Companion Animals. An Aburame character doesn't need to choose anything, their Kikaichū Colony feature adds itself to Companion Animals automatically the moment Apply Clan Traits is hit, since every Aburame has one from birth.

Once Summoning Technique (Kuchiyose no Jutsu) has been added to your Jutsu list, each Summoning Animal/Group or Other Bonded Animal entry in Companion Animals also unlocks a quick Roll Summoning Technique button right there in the section, a shortcut so you don't have to jump back to the Jutsu list every time you want to summon that companion. It rolls Ninjutsu, exactly like using the jutsu normally, it's just a faster way to get there. This shortcut is not available on Ninken entries, since a ninken partner isn't called through Summoning Jutsu in the first place, it's simply already there.

Flying Thunder God Technique (Hiraishin)

The Flying Thunder God Technique works in two separate steps, marking and teleporting, backed by two separate jutsu in the Jutsu Library.

  • 1. The mark: after adding Flying Thunder God Formula to your Jutsu list, roll Fūinjutsu to apply the seal to a person, object, or surface. This creates a permanent teleport marker at that point.
  • 2. Throwing a marked kunai: if the formula was inscribed onto a kunai meant to be thrown, such as a Flying Thunder God Kunai, roll Shurikenjutsu to see whether the throw successfully plants it where intended.
  • 3. Multiple marks: a user can maintain many marks at once, not just one, and can freely choose among them as teleport destinations.
  • 4. Marks on people: if a target is marked, the user can reach them at any time by warping straight to the mark.
  • 5. No physical path: the technique doesn't cross the space between the two points, it instantly transfers the user through space-time to the mark. This isn't a technique that moves fast, it displaces.
  • 6. Transferring others: the same principle can move other people or objects to a marked point, not just the user.
  • 7. Fundamental limit: a mark must already be in place. This is not free teleportation to any arbitrarily chosen coordinate, only to an existing seal.
  • 8. The teleport: once a mark exists, add Flying Thunder God Technique (Hiraishin) to your Jutsu list and roll Ninjutsu to actually perform the warp to that mark.
  • 9. What it doesn't do: the technique only teleports you. Any attack, jutsu, or action performed immediately after arriving is a separate roll from the Flying Thunder God Technique itself, the jutsu just gets you there.

Recap, with a kunai: Fūinjutsu (apply the formula) → Shurikenjutsu (throw the kunai) → Ninjutsu (perform the teleport).
Recap, without a kunai (marking a surface or person directly by touch): Fūinjutsu (apply the formula) → Ninjutsu (perform the teleport).

Inventory & Item Usage

Your Inventory represents the items your character actually has available in the story. If a technique or action requires a physical item, you must have that item in your Inventory before you can use it. For example, to use Shurikenjutsu to throw a kunai, shuriken, senbon, or another thrown weapon, the appropriate item must be present in your Inventory. Roll Shurikenjutsu to determine how well the projectile is thrown, with the final outcome judged by the DM according to the normal rules.

After using a ninja tool or consumable, click Use on that item in the Inventory. This consumes exactly one unit. For example, if you have 2 Shuriken, using one reduces the quantity to 1. The Use button appears only for Ninja Tools and Consumables; Scrolls and Miscellaneous items are not consumed this way.

This tracking is intentionally simple, but it is also optional as a table preference. If counting every individual kunai, shuriken, senbon, or similar tool becomes too tedious, the DM and players can instead treat the character as carrying a large narrative stockpile of that item. In that style, keep one inventory entry to represent the supply rather than tracking every individual piece, and occasionally handle resupply in roleplay by having the ninja visit a ninja tool shop or otherwise replenish their stock. Choose whichever approach keeps the game fun without turning the character sheet into an accounting ledger.

Scrolls work differently: a Summoning Scroll does not need to be consumed when you use the Summoning Jutsu. Once the contract has been successfully created, the scroll remains in your Inventory and functions passively whenever you perform that corresponding Summoning Jutsu.

Perception

Perception covers the ordinary stuff, spotting an ambush, noticing something's off, catching a detail in a crowded room, using either your eyes or plain ninja instinct. But it scales further than that: at high levels, a character built around Perception should start to feel like a proper sensory-type shinobi, someone who reads a fight through chakra, sound, or scent instead of (or alongside) sight. Canonically, the Second Hokage, Tobirama Senju, could place a hand on the ground and sense precise chakra signatures, counts, and locations from vast distances, that's the kind of thing a high Perception roll can represent for a sensory-focused character. Depending on the character, a Perception roll might mean listening for footsteps, sensing raw chakra the way a dedicated sensor-nin does, or smelling out a hidden or approaching person the way an Inuzuka would: smell is simply the sense responsible for perceiving odor-based stimuli, so tracking someone by scent, picking their trail out of a crowd, or noticing them before they're seen falls squarely under Perception too.

Perception also governs how well a character uses an active Dōjutsu: for the Byakugan, it determines how far and how precisely you see, whether that's spotting movement at range or reading a target's chakra through walls. For the Sharingan, it determines how well you predict an opponent's next move (see the Tomoe rule below). Chakra Perception, actually seeing chakra itself, whether as an aura, a flow, or a visible technique forming, also falls under this same roll; Sasuke reading Deidara's chakra mid-battle without an active Dōjutsu is a good example of Perception at work, not a dedicated dōjutsu ability.

Insight

Insight is intuition, not observation: it's for reading a person, not scanning a room. It covers sensing someone's true intentions, catching a lie, or picking up on nervous or suspicious behavior. In a fight, that same intuition scales up to reading a whole enemy squad rather than one person: which one looks like the leader, who's playing support, who's covering someone else, which techniques are being used in combination, and which member of the team is actually the dangerous one. A strong Insight roll should read like a behavioral read, not a stat block: "That one isn't trying to kill you, they're keeping their distance and covering the other, they're probably playing support" is the kind of thing a good roll gets you, not "that one secretly has poisoned kunai." Insight also covers predicting a specific move from someone the character actually knows, rather than reading a stranger's body language: "I know this guy, I've fought him before, he always follows that combo with a Substitution," or "he used that same jutsu right after this exact setup last time," is Insight drawing on personal history and familiarity with a person, as opposed to Tactics, which reads a pattern out of pure in-the-moment battlefield analysis, even against a total stranger.

A useful distinction: Insight is not Perception. "I look closely at the table for anything strange" is Perception. "I look at the table and try to work out if someone tampered with the scene" is Insight, since the question is about interpreting behavior or clues, not just spotting them. Insight also shouldn't hand out information the character couldn't reasonably deduce, a great roll doesn't mean "I know the merchant is secretly an assassin," it means the DM can give a much sharper read on how the merchant is behaving.

The same logic separates Insight from other rolls when there's something more concrete to notice. If the party might be walking into a trap: Insight is the gut feeling that something's off; Perception is actually spotting the trap, whether through plain eyesight, a Byakugan's 360° vision, or a sensor-nin's range; and a dedicated skill like Traps is recognizing it on sight because the character has personally set or disarmed dozens of shuriken and paper-bomb traps just like it, pure experience talking. All three can apply to the very same trap, they just represent different reasons for noticing it.

Insight also covers interrogating someone, for example a prisoner captured mid-battle: roll it to work out whether they're lying, stalling for time, holding something back, or actually close to breaking under pressure. Just like the rest of Insight, a strong roll gets you a behavioral read, not free information, it tells you how to push and what their reaction gives away, not the secret itself handed over outright.

Tactics

Tactics is Battle IQ: not how well a character fights, but how well they read a fight, predict what's coming, and turn a bad position into a good one. Roll it whenever a character makes a meaningful tactical call, rather than just swinging at something. A few of the ways it comes up:

  • Reading the battlefield: roll Tactics to quickly pick out things like favorable positions, escape routes, ambush points, terrain worth exploiting, useful obstacles, weak points in an enemy formation, or how to best split up the battlefield between your team. Six shinobi in a forest: a good roll might reveal that the trees break their sightlines, and splitting into two groups would force them to fight separately instead of as a unit.
  • Reading an enemy's combat style: arguably the skill's core use, and it isn't limited to before a fight starts. Tactics keeps working mid-fight: after watching an enemy use a technique two or three times, a player can ask, "We've seen this technique three times now, what can I work out about it?" and roll Tactics to find out. A strong result lets the DM reveal a real tactical detail, not a random fact: a repeated setup or pattern, the wind-up before the technique lands, a dependency on an ally to pull it off, its effective range, a narrative cooldown before it can be used again, a preferred position the enemy keeps returning to, a condition that has to be met before they can use it at all, or a piece of predictable behavior they keep falling back on. This isn't magically knowing the jutsu itself, it's field analysis: reading the fight in front of you, not reading a wiki page.
  • Commanding a team: a character with strong Tactics can call out a plan that actually improves coordination, "you keep him busy, you get the Water Release (Suiton) ready, I'll push him toward you." The roll determines how well that plan holds up; a strong result might grant advantage on a follow-up roll, a situational bonus, a safe reposition, a coordinated reaction, an opening against an enemy, reduced risk on an action, or useful information for an ally.
  • Setting up jutsu combos: recognizing that Wind Release (Fūton) used just before Fire Release (Katon) will fan the flames and extend their range, or that sending a Shadow Clone (Kage Bunshin) in first lets an ally react with Substitution (Kawarimi no Jutsu) while a trap triggers behind it, is Tactics predicting a sequence, the jutsu themselves aren't any stronger for it.
  • Countering a smart enemy: when an opponent does something clever, a Tactics roll is the "wait, I see what they're doing, can I answer it?" moment. A strong result gives a concrete tactical read, not a flat bonus: "Don't attack the two in front, they're a distraction, the real target is the one behind them about to finish a jutsu." This is the Shikamaru-style use of the skill, and it works just as well one-on-one, a Shikamaru-versus-Temari style duel, as it does for a whole squad.

Tactics doesn't replace other skills, it works alongside them. As a rule of thumb: noticing someone hidden is Perception, sensing that a person is nervous or lying is Insight, recognizing a jutsu you're seeing is Chakra Knowledge, and figuring out how to actually exploit that jutsu in the fight is Tactics.

Weapons

Weapons is the skill you roll to fight with any weapon, whether it is a sword, a bow or a puppet. When you attack with a weapon, roll Weapons, and based on the result the DM decides how much damage your weapon did.

Chakra-conductive weapons: some weapons are made of a special material that conducts chakra, like the Chakra Blades. With these, you can roll Weapons first and then Ninjutsu, to apply chakra to the weapon. Based on the results of the two rolls, the DM decides how much damage you did to the enemy.

Applying a chakra nature: you can also apply a special chakra nature to your weapon, again by rolling Ninjutsu after Weapons. For example, if you know Lightning Release, you roll Weapons first (it represents your skill with the weapon) and then Ninjutsu (it represents applying the lightning chakra), and you can narrate that the blade is now surrounded by lightning and electricity, so it does more damage. It always depends on the numbers you rolled and the DM's judgement.

Puppets

Puppets are weapons: in Naruto, puppets are classified and used as complex, long-range ninja weapons and mobile arsenals, not as ordinary toys or independent beings. For that reason puppets are considered weapons in this game, so you roll Weapons to fight with them, precisely because a puppet is a weapon that also contains other weapons.

  • Walking arsenals: puppets are packed with hidden mechanical weaponry, such as poison-coated blades, senbon launchers, shuriken and smoke bombs.
  • Chakra-controlled equipment: they are moved remotely through invisible chakra threads extending from the user's fingers, so they work as extensions of the shinobi's own limbs and combat gear.
  • Storage in scrolls: because they are treated as standard tactical gear, puppeteers usually seal their puppets inside scrolls with fūinjutsu, for quick deployment and transport.
  • Human puppets: master puppeteers can turn human corpses into specialized puppets, keeping their original chakra flows and kekkei genkai abilities, to use as living-grade combat tools. Making one takes a Medicine roll, because you have to remove the organs and blood from the body and add preservatives so it won't decay before it can become a puppet.

Fighting through a puppet is a two-part check, reflecting both the physical skill of the puppeteer and the chakra threads used to control the puppet:

  • Roll Weapons (Dexterity): represents the puppeteer's physical skill at maneuvering and fighting through the puppet, the same way any other weapon check would.
  • Roll Chakra Threads (Puppet Technique): made after the Weapons roll, you roll the technique itself, which uses the skill Chakra Control and represents the precision needed to manage the chakra threads that actually move the puppet. Add it to your Jutsu list from the Jutsu Library, where you find it in the Non-Elemental section.
  • Chakra cost: operating a puppet drains chakra. The DM sets whether the cost is paid once per use of the puppet or once per turn it stays active, depending on the campaign's rules.

Nindo

Every character has a Nindo, a personal creed or way of the ninja, regardless of clan. Write yours in the Nindo (Ninja Way / Personal Way) field under Character Details, it can be a single line like "I never go back on my word" or a longer personal philosophy.

Nindo isn't a fixed dice bonus, it's a roleplay lever you can pull at the table. When a moment fits your creed, tell your DM: "This is my Nindo, [your creed], so I want to [get back up, push through the pain, refuse to leave them behind, etc.]," and ask for a reroll or a favorable ruling on the spot. The DM decides how much weight to give it, the same way they judge everything else in this system: by how well it fits the character and the story. This applies equally to a clan character and a Non-Clan character, nobody gets more or less Nindo for having (or not having) a bloodline.

Proficiencies

A proficiency marks a skill a character is meaningfully better at than the rest, the handful of things they've genuinely specialized in rather than just dabbled with, which is why it comes with a bonus on rolls using that skill. If a player picks Insight, Tactics, and Perception as their three proficiencies, that character reads people, battles, and their surroundings noticeably better than they do most other things, and their rolls in those three areas reflect it. Proficiencies represent specific training, areas of expertise, and general skills a character has mastered through class, background, clan, or natural aptitude. The standard recommendation is 3 proficiencies per character, but this is not hard-locked; a DM may allow more or fewer proficiencies depending on the campaign's rules. The in-roleplay reason behind a proficiency can come from anywhere: a character proficient in Taijutsu might have earned it through years of hard training, or might simply have a natural, almost genetic gift for close combat inherited from their family, either explanation works, it's just flavor for how the character got there.

Dōjutsu

  • Byakugan: roll Perception (Wisdom) to use it. Active, it grants near 360° vision and lets the user see chakra flowing through a target's body, including the network of chakra pathways that carries it. Along those pathways are tenketsu, tiny chakra points where chakra can be released or disrupted. The Byakugan lets the user see these points with exceptional precision, even through solid matter, allowing Gentle Fist strikes to target and interfere with an opponent's chakra network rather than simply striking the body.
  • Sharingan: roll Perception (Wisdom) to use it. Active, the Sharingan sharpens your ability to read an opponent's movement before it happens and lets you visually follow the flow of chakra through a technique or a body. Because it can read that chakra flow, it also gives you a narrative ability to copy a jutsu you witness and grasp how the technique is being performed. Seeing how a jutsu works is not the same as truly mastering it, however: copying it does not teach you the underlying chakra nature change the technique requires, and Kekkei Genkai techniques can never be copied this way, no matter how well you understand them. A freshly copied technique should also feel unfamiliar in your hands, coming out clumsy, inefficient, or exhausting to use until you actually train it, just as copying a taijutsu style on sight does not give you the physical conditioning needed to perform it cleanly. The Sharingan's tomoe represent more than a bonus to perception: each Tomoe adds +1 to your Perception roll, while the eye itself becomes more capable as it matures. 1 Tomoe: You can read an opponent's telegraphed movement a beat before it happens. 2 Tomoe: You can copy a jutsu you witness, if your DM allows a Chakra Knowledge check to pull it off. 3 Tomoe: Your Sharingan has matured enough to cast genjutsu on its own. You can roll Genjutsu to affect a target through eye contact alone, with no hand seals needed, trapping them in an illusion or manipulating their senses. Although this basic Sharingan genjutsu is far weaker than the signature techniques of an awakened Mangekyō Sharingan, such as Itachi's Tsukuyomi, which can distort a victim's perception of time and reality on an entirely different level, it is still a dangerous ability in its own right. Any shinobi who meets your gaze risks being caught in an illusion before they even realize what happened.
  • Mangekyō Sharingan: depends on the ability being used, for example Kamui uses Chakra Control and Tsukuyomi uses Genjutsu, and so on. Each eye can awaken its own distinct Mangekyō ability, independent of the other, tracked separately as Left Eye and Right Eye in the Uchiha Clan section. Awakening the Mangekyō in both eyes additionally unlocks Susanoo, a gigantic avatar of chakra, which is not picked directly but is added to your Jutsu list automatically once both eye choices are made and Apply Clan Traits is hit. Unlock this in roleplay, not at character creation: a brand-new Uchiha genin, fresh out of the Academy, has not awakened the Mangekyō Sharingan. Leave the Mangekyō Sharingan — Left Eye / Right Eye choices on (No Choice Made) in the Uchiha Clan section until your character actually awakens one, through a real, DM-approved story moment, then go back to the Clan section, make the selection, and hit Apply Clan Traits to add it to your character for real.
  • Rinnegan: roll Chakra Control when actively using its powers. The Rinnegan is exceptionally rare and should never be treated as a normal character-creation option. Before taking it, the player must have a strong, specific roleplay reason approved by the DM, such as an exceptionally rare awakening, a legitimate transplant, or another story-earned origin. The eye can perceive chakra and its flow, see certain otherwise hidden chakra constructs, and gives access to the Six Paths family of powers: Deva, Asura, Human, Animal, Preta, Naraka, and the Outer Path. Those powers are not all assumed to be mastered immediately. The DM should decide which applications the character has actually learned or unlocked through roleplay. User-specific abilities such as Sasuke's Amenotejikara or Madara's Limbo are likewise not automatically granted to every Rinnegan wielder.

Clan Bloodlines

Clan Function

Clans work on two levels at once, and it helps to know which is which. The first is mechanical: each clan grants a fixed Ability Score Increase in a specific area tied to what that bloodline is known for. Akimichi, for example, are built to be physically massive, so they get +2 Constitution and +1 Strength (a bonus on top of the normal 27-point pool). That part is a hard number, applied automatically when you hit Apply Clan Traits.

Everything else written in a clan's section, the traits and features beyond that Ability Score Increase, is mostly narrative power, not programmed mechanics. It exists to give a character something concrete to lean on in the story, not a new button to press. An Akimichi player can point at their clan's size and strength to justify rolling Strength to shove a boulder off a road. A Hoshigaki player can point at their clan's "Call of the Deep" feature to justify breathing underwater or calling sharks to their side mid-fight, without needing a separate mechanical rule for it. The text is the permission slip: it tells the table what your character can plausibly attempt and why, so the DM has something concrete to say yes to. Keep an eye on your own clan's features and bring them up at the table, they're meant to be used, not just read once and forgotten.

There's another practical perk to picking a specific clan: many of them start the campaign with a chakra nature already marked as Known, an Uchiha, for instance, starts already knowing Fire Release. That's a narrative shortcut as much as a mechanical one, your character gets to walk into the story already able to say "I already know this nature," with no need to seek out training to unlock it first, unlike a character who has to earn that chakra nature over the course of play.

Clan Dojutsu

Each clan's traits and jutsu in the Character Sheet's Clan section are meant to represent someone born into that clan. Dōjutsu are a special case, and stricter than ordinary jutsu: the Sharingan and Byakugan are physical eyes, not techniques that can be trained into existence. A non-Uchiha simply does not have the Sharingan sitting latent in their body waiting to be unlocked, and a non-Hyuga does not have a Byakugan waiting to awaken, no amount of training, chakra control, or study lets a shinobi grow either eye from scratch. Possessing the eye itself is the bloodline. That said, dōjutsu have changed hands before, Kakashi famously fights with a Sharingan he wasn't born with, received from Obito. If a player wants their character to use a Dojutsu outside their own clan, work out the in-story reason together (an inherited eye, a stolen eye from a defeated Uchiha/Hyuga opponent, or whatever fits the campaign) rather than treating Clan Dojutsu as strictly off-limits. The Clan section's Access tab is there to track exactly this kind of exception.

Clan Affinity

A clan affinity is the chakra nature a clan is known for and commonly builds its techniques around. A character born into that clan starts with that chakra nature marked as Known, applying clan traits handles this automatically when the clan defines one. What that affinity actually represents can vary from clan to clan. For some, like the Uchiha with Fire Release, Nara with Yin Release, or Akimichi with Yang Release, it's a matter of tradition: generations of the clan have trained in that nature and built a whole fighting style around it, but the nature itself isn't exclusive, any shinobi could, in theory, learn Fire Release with enough training. For others, the affinity is the bloodline itself, a true Kekkei Genkai. Bakuton's Explosion Release is the clearest example: it isn't just a tradition the clan leans on, it's a chakra nature that, per the setting's usual logic, only someone actually born with the Bakuton bloodline can use at all, no amount of outside training unlocks it.

Clan Techniques

Some clans have techniques reserved for them in the Jutsu Library, like the Uchiha's Great Fireball Technique. This means the technique was invented by, or is a defining tradition of, that clan, so it's mostly used by and associated with that clan's members. It does not mean the technique is inherently unlearnable by anyone else. A Fire Release technique is still just Fire Release under the hood, so a non-Uchiha with Fire Release known could technically train to learn it too, it's just understood in-world as a specific Uchiha clan technique, so a non-Uchiha would effectively be learning an Uchiha technique, and would need an in-story reason to do so (an Uchiha teacher, a stolen scroll, whatever fits the campaign).

The same logic applies to Kekkei Genkai techniques. A Yuki clan Ice Release technique is still built from Water and Wind Release underneath, so a non-Yuki character who somehow possesses the Ice Release Kekkei Genkai (through a rare bloodline mix, a transplant, or another strong in-story justification) could technically learn it, even though it's a Yuki clan technique by tradition and origin.

Hebi work the same way. Non-Hebi can learn Hebi snake techniques with the right teacher or justification, Orochimaru himself taught various snake-related techniques to Anko, Kabuto, and Sasuke in canon, which they use.

Clan Skill Proficiencies

Clan skill proficiencies shown in the Character Sheet are suggestions only, not automatic mechanical bonuses. They describe skills that naturally fit members of the clan, but applying Clan Traits must never mark those skills as proficient, and clan suggestions do not stack with or overwrite class, background, or player-chosen proficiencies. Actual proficiencies should be chosen or assigned through the character's other sources, such as class, background, or player choice. For example, Animal Handling is a natural recommendation for an Inuzuka or Aburame, but it only becomes a real proficiency if another rule or the player actually assigns it.

Clan Appearance

Each clan's "Clan Appearance" trait describes what members of that clan tend to look like, common features passed down within the bloodline, not a mandatory look every member must have. It's there as a starting point or a piece of flavor to lean on, not a rule: a player is always free to describe their character's appearance differently, a clan member who doesn't fit the mold is perfectly normal and doesn't need special justification.

Homebrew Clans

Homebrew Clans are clans that do not exist in the canon Naruto setting, but are created specifically for this game. As a player, you are also free to create your own clan for the sake of character concept and fun, including its own signature jutsu, unique abilities, Kekkei Genkai or other bloodline traits, customs, appearance, and history. A homebrew clan should still feel like it belongs in Naruto: its abilities, power level, themes, and history should fit the setting and remain consistent with the rules and tone of your campaign. The goal is not to replace canon, but to give players room to fill narrative gaps and create characters that could plausibly exist within the world. Your DM has the final say on whether a custom clan and its abilities fit the campaign.

Hebi, Bakuton, Tenshi, and Jorōgumo are examples of this approach. None of these clans are canon Naruto clans, but each one solves a useful narrative problem for a player who wants to build a character around a very distinctive fighting style associated with a canon character.

Bakuton exists to give an in-world origin to characters built around Deidara's explosive style. Canon Naruto shows Deidara using Explosion Release and the two additional mouths in his palms, but it never gives us a clear clan or hereditary explanation for why he was born with those traits. In this game, the Bakuton bloodline fills that gap: a Bakuton character is born with the physical traits and innate Explosion Release affinity that define the clan. Their extra mouths, explosive chakra, and clay-based fighting style are not abilities that appeared from nowhere; they are the inherited foundation of the character.

Hebi serves the same purpose for characters inspired by Orochimaru. Canon Naruto gives Orochimaru an extensive collection of snake-based techniques and unusual body abilities, but it does not establish a canon clan that naturally explains an entire character built around that style. The Hebi clan fills that narrative space by making serpent-based techniques a defining part of the clan's identity. These techniques are therefore primarily associated with Hebi characters, giving a snake-themed character a coherent origin and making the style feel like a deliberate clan tradition rather than a pile of unrelated techniques. This does not mean a non-Hebi can never learn them. Orochimaru canonically teaches snake techniques to people such as Anko, Kabuto, and Sasuke, and those characters can use them despite not being Hebi. Under this game's logic, a non-Hebi can therefore learn Hebi techniques when a Hebi member teaches them or another strong in-story justification exists. What remains unique to Hebi is their natural affinity with the style: a Hebi can manifest certain serpent techniques without relying on normal hand-seal use, including the ability to send snakes from the mouth, echoing Orochimaru's fighting style even when his hands were unavailable. That mouth-based expression is a Hebi feature and is not available to a non-Hebi who merely learned the technique.

Tenshi fills a similar gap for players who want to play a character inspired by Konan. Konan's paper techniques are canonically her own developed ninjutsu, not the result of a canon Paper Release Kekkei Genkai or a known paper bloodline. She developed her style around origami and paper from childhood. Technically, that means another shinobi could learn paper-based ninjutsu with sufficient training. For this game, however, the Tenshi clan gives that concept a coherent hereditary foundation so that a character whose entire combat identity revolves around paper does not feel mechanically or narratively disconnected from their origin. Tenshi possess a unique non-elemental bloodline that gives their bodies and chakra a natural affinity for paper, allowing them to manipulate it, transform their bodies through paper-based techniques, and build their entire fighting style around the material. The result is not claiming that Tenshi are canon Naruto lore; it is deliberately filling a canon gap so a Konan-inspired character can have a clear reason for being born with and specializing in that style.

Jorōgumo fills the gap for players who want to play a character built around a distinctive spider-ninja fighting style. The Jorōgumo clan gives it a hereditary answer: a clan of spider-ninja from Otogakure, the Village Hidden in the Sound, whose bodies naturally produce chakra-laced silk and Sticky Gold, whose members can be born with extra arms and a third eye, and who bond with spiders. Like Tenshi, they are a non-elemental hiden style and not a canon Kekkei Genkai, so a Jorōgumo's spider abilities are the inherited foundation of the character and not powers that appeared from nowhere. A non-Jorōgumo can still learn these techniques when there is a strong in-story reason, but the spider body itself is what remains unique to the clan.

This is the underlying purpose of these homebrew clans: they turn a recognizable character concept into an understandable in-world origin. Deidara-style characters are born Bakuton, Orochimaru-style characters are born Hebi, Konan-style characters are born Tenshi, and spider-ninja characters are born Jorōgumo. Their defining traits therefore have a place in their personal history and family lineage. The clans are not meant to say that Naruto canon secretly contained these clans; they are a game-design tool that expands the setting while preserving the feeling and internal logic of Naruto.

Semi-Homebrew Clans

Semi-Homebrew Clans are clans that are technically canon, but about which we do not know enough to put them in the game exactly as they are. Their existence or their name comes from canon, but a lot of what is written in their clan list is not canon: it was added by this game to give the clan more flavor, and to make it playable, fun and not inferior to the other clans. Every semi-homebrew clan is marked with [Semi-Homebrew] in its description. Having them in the game gives players a unique playstyle that no other clan gives.

Jūgo: Jūgo's clan exists in canon, but its name is unknown, and we know very little about it beyond its members' ability to absorb natural energy and their surges of madness. In this game it is called the Jūgo clan, and it lets you build a Jūgo-style character.

Kazekage: canon has a clan of Sunagakure with high authority, from which several Kazekage came, but its real name is unknown. It is known to be skilled in Wind Release, with some members inheriting Magnet Release. In this game it is called the Kazekage clan, and it lets you play a Gaara-style character with Magnet Release and sand.

Terumī: the surname Terumī exists in canon, but nobody says whether a Terumī clan exists, or why Mei Terumī has two kekkei genkai, Lava Release and Boil Release. Creating the clan gives that a reason, and lets someone play a character like her who uses both natures.

What is canon and what is not: the names (Jūgo, Kazekage, Terumī) and the few things we know about these clans are canon. Some of their features are not: they were added to give the clan flavor. They try to follow canon by taking traits of a canon character and giving them to the whole clan. For example, a calm Jūgo loves animals and talks to them. That is canon for Jūgo, but we don't know if it is his personal trait or a trait of his clan, so in this game it is a clan feature. That makes it semi-canon.

Why "semi": they are not fully homebrew, because they keep a canon name and canon information, and they are not fully canon, because part of their features is added. Treat the canon parts as canon, and the rest as the flavor that makes the clan work at the table.

Passive Abilities

Some abilities are not a one-off technique but a state you enter and stay in. These are passive abilities, and the main ones are Sage Mode, the Curse Mark, the Jūgo clan's Sage Transformation and a Jinchūriki's Chakra Cloak and Nine-Tails Chakra Mode. You roll once to enter the state and its chakra is spent then, and from that moment the state remains on your body until you end it or lose it. While you are in it, the DM should be kind to your rolls that the state would obviously help, such as Strength, Ninjutsu, Taijutsu, Genjutsu, Perception or Chakra Control, usually a passive +5 or more according to the DM's own rules and how strong the state is. Every now and then the DM can ask you to roll Chakra Control to see if you can still hold it. If you fail and lose the state, the kindness is gone with it. The stronger the state, the harder it is to hold, so the DM can ask more often. Nothing here changes a number on your sheet, it is the DM's judgement of what your state deserves.

Sage Mode

Sage Mode is senjutsu, the art of gathering the natural energy of the world and blending it with your own chakra into senjutsu chakra. It has its own section in the Jutsu Library, and it is a passive ability, so the rules above apply. In canon it works like this.

What it needs: Senjutsu is taught by animal sages in remote sacred places, after long and difficult training to feel natural energy. In canon these teachers are the toads, the snakes and the slugs, and you can follow one of them. You can also create your own: invent a special place with a special animal that isn't canon, such as a place with a rabbit sage who teaches you, in roleplay, to become a sage. It only has to keep the same senjutsu theme as canon: a remote sacred place, animals in tune with natural energy, and long training to feel it. Whichever animal teaches you is the one your Sage Mode takes after: if you can't control the energy well, you start turning into that animal (see What it costs below). The user needs a very large chakra reserve of their own and a body strong enough to bear the extra power. It is not for a starting character: your DM should only allow it after a real story of training and a teacher who accepts you.

Entering it: Natural energy can only be gathered by staying completely still, which leaves you vulnerable, so entering Sage Mode in the middle of a fight is nearly impossible alone. In canon this is solved by having someone else, or clones, gather the energy for you. Your physical, spiritual and natural energy must be balanced perfectly.

What it gives: Your strength, speed, stamina, reflexes and durability rise dramatically, and your ninjutsu, genjutsu and taijutsu become more powerful. The natural energy acts as an extension of your body, so your attacks reach farther than they look. You also sense the chakra around you, so it is very hard to surprise you. Sensor types can feel senjutsu chakra, and it noticeably changes your chakra signature. This is exactly why the DM should be generous with your rolls while you are in Sage Mode: your strength, speed, stamina, reflexes and durability rise dramatically, your ninjutsu, genjutsu and taijutsu become more powerful, and the natural energy extends your attacks farther than they look, so the state deserves the passive +5 or more described in Passive Abilities.

What it costs: Senjutsu chakra can't be replenished while you move, so you can't stay in Sage Mode for long, and when it ends you are left exhausted. If you take in too much natural energy you begin to turn into the animal that taught you, which can still be reversed by expelling the energy, but if the transformation finishes you turn to stone, permanently. If you can't balance the energies perfectly you keep some traits of the animal that taught you while in the mode, such as toad-like features for a toad's student or long ears for a rabbit's. Some sages can only gather enough energy for a single technique, not to fill their whole body, and that isn't Sage Mode.

How to run it: Rolling Sage Mode spends its chakra and the state remains. The DM can ask for a Chakra Control roll to see if you still keep your balance. If you fail, the mode ends, and if you failed badly or ignored the warnings, the DM can begin the transformation into an animal. That is a narrative danger, not a number.

Sage Arts: The techniques in the Senjutsu section whose names start with Sage Art are ninjutsu boosted by senjutsu chakra, and they can only be used while you are in Sage Mode. If you lose the mode, you lose them until you enter it again.

Sage Arts and their normal versions: Many Sage Arts are stronger versions of a normal technique. For example, the Big Ball Rasengan is a normal technique that you can use at any time, while the Sage Art: Big Ball Rasengan is a stronger version that you can only use while in Sage Mode. They are two separate jutsu. Knowing one does not give you the other: you add each of them to your Jutsu list on its own, and your character must have learned each one in the story. Other Sage Arts are completely original jutsu, not the sage version of an existing technique, such as the Sage Art: Goemon. They have no normal version behind them, they can only be used while in Sage Mode, and you add them to your Jutsu list on their own too.

The exception: The Sage Art: Amphibian Technique is the only Sage Art you can use before you are in Sage Mode, because its purpose is to help you enter and keep it. An animal sage merges onto your shoulders and gathers natural energy for you, so you don't have to stand still. It needs a sage partner.

Curse Mark

The Curse Mark is a cursed seal that changes your body, and it is a passive ability, so the rules above apply. It sits in the Senjutsu section because it was created from senjutsu chakra. It is applied by a bite on the neck from someone who knows the technique, and the recipient is left unconscious while their body accepts or rejects it. Only about one body in ten is compatible, so a character who receives it risks dying: let your DM decide the outcome through the story or a roll, and don't take it at character creation without a backstory approved by them. If you survive, you can call on the seal's power boosts, and your access grows through repeated use, so the DM can be kinder to your rolls as you master it. If you never use it, the seal grows dormant, and whoever made it can force it awake if they can reach you.

Sage Transformation

The Sage Transformation is the Jūgo clan's own version of Sage Mode, and it is a passive ability, so the rules above apply. Unlike Sage Mode, it doesn't need stillness, a teacher or an animal sage: the natural energy is already in your body, so you can enter it almost instantly and reshape your body, whole or in part, into weapons and appendages. You also get the same boost to strength, speed, stamina, reflexes, perception and durability, which is why the DM should be generous with your rolls while you are in it. The price is the clan's rage: your body keeps absorbing natural energy, which brings sudden surges of madness, and in a surge you are not acting on your own will, so the DM decides how much havoc you cause. Techniques marked as needing the Sage Transformation can only be used while you are in it.

Jinchūriki

Jinchūriki Function

A Jinchūriki is someone who carries a tailed beast sealed inside them. To become one, pick One-Tail through Nine-Tails in the Jinchūriki list under Character Details (it defaults to Non-Jinchūriki). Only then does a collapsible Jinchūriki section appear under Clan. Like a clan, it is mostly narrative: the text tells the table what your bond with the beast plausibly lets you attempt. Unlike a clan, it gives no Ability Score Increase, no suggested proficiencies, no skill bonuses and no automatic chakra changes. Nobody is better at a skill just for hosting a beast, they simply have access to its power.

Bonding

Choose one of three Bonding levels: Uncontrolled Beast, Peaceful Coexistence or Fully Controlled. It changes no number, it decides how much of the beast you can actually steer, above all what happens with the Chakra Cloak. Bonding is meant to grow through roleplay, the way Naruto and Kurama's did: when your story earns it, change the level and tell your DM.

Uncontrolled Beast: the cloak is not yours to call. It comes out on its own when you are enraged (or grieving, or near death), and when it does you lose control: you are no longer acting on your own will, and the DM decides how much havoc you cause in that enraged form. You can only try to suppress it with Chakra Control. A full transformation or a Tailed Beast Ball is never your choice at this level.

Peaceful Coexistence: you and the beast have a truce. You can call the cloak and dismiss it yourself, but a surge of anger or fear can still push it further than you meant, and going beyond what the beast offers risks slipping back into an uncontrolled state. A full transformation or a Tailed Beast Ball is possible only with the beast's consent and in short bursts.

Fully Controlled: you can call the cloak and use it without going enraged, and a full transformation or a Tailed Beast Ball is your choice. Even so, if you become enraged the cloak may still appear on its own.

Passive Jinchūriki Abilities

Some Jinchūriki abilities are not one-off techniques but a state you stay in, above all the Chakra Cloak (and, for the Nine-Tails, the Nine-Tails Chakra Mode). When you are in control of it (Fully Controlled), rolling the Chakra Cloak spends its chakra, and the cloak then wraps your body and remains. While you are surrounded by the beast's chakra, the DM may be kinder to your rolls, such as Strength or Ninjutsu, adding a passive +5 or more, according to the DM's own rules and your state. The same applies when the cloak comes out on its own because you are enraged: even a low roll deserves a kinder reading, since you are wrapped in the beast's chakra. Every now and then the DM can ask you to roll Chakra Control to see if you can still hold the cloak. If you fail and lose the cloak, the passive bonus is gone until it returns. Holding it is hard, so at the start it will mostly appear only when you are enraged, which means you can't use it as a reliable way to roll high. How far the cloak transforms you, from a thin shroud to a beast-shaped mantle with tails, has no fixed stages: it is all roleplay, and the DM decides it from the moment and your Bonding. The Nine-Tails Chakra Mode works the same way: it is a passive ability that stays on you once you enter it, the DM can be just as kind to your rolls while you are in it, and it can be lost by failing the Chakra Control check to hold it.

Apply Jinchūriki

Hitting Apply Jinchūriki (a Bonding level must be chosen first) adds a Chakra Cloak and the Tailed Beast Ball, plus the beast's own canon techniques to your Jutsu list, and marks the beast's chakra natures as Known (see below). Only canon abilities are included, though some of them are anime-only. They are not in the Jutsu Library, this is the only way to get them. Chakra costs are suggestions, as always. The jutsu stay on your sheet if you later change the list, so delete them by hand if the beast is removed or resealed elsewhere. Your Chakra pool is never touched automatically: if your table wants a bigger reserve for a Jinchūriki, raise Chakra per Level in the Chakra section (see Chakra Consumption).

Beasts and Chakra Natures

Each beast comes with the chakra natures it is tied to, and hitting Apply Jinchūriki marks them as Known automatically, so you have them from the start, like a clan's starting nature. Shukaku gives you Wind and Earth, plus Magnet Release for its sand. Son Gokū gives you Lava, along with the Fire and Earth it is made from. Kokuō gives you Boil, along with Fire and Water. Matatabi gives you Fire, and Isobu and Saiken give you Water. Chōmei, Gyūki and Kurama grant no nature. Applying only ever adds natures and never removes one, so if the beast is removed, adjust your Known natures by hand. The techniques of those natures still come from the Jutsu Library, and the DM has the final word.

Nine-Tails Chakra Mode

The Nine-Tails Chakra Mode is a form that only the Nine-Tails jinchūriki can ever reach. No other tailed beast grants a chakra mode, the other jinchūriki have the Chakra Cloak and their beast's own techniques. It is also a passive ability, like the Chakra Cloak (see Passive Jinchūriki Abilities). It is not available at the start: a genin can't begin with it, it has to be earned through roleplay. In canon, Naruto reached it only after training with a jinchūriki who had already tamed a beast, entering his own mind, facing the hatred that fed on his anger, and fighting Kurama there until he took its chakra with his mother's help, after which the two became real partners. Your story should ask for something of the same weight: a guide, a confrontation with the beast inside your mind, and the moment the beast agrees to work with you. When your DM decides you have earned it, open the Jinchūriki section, set Nine-Tails Chakra Mode to Unlocked and hit Apply Jinchūriki to add it to your Jutsu list. It should come with Peaceful Coexistence or Fully Controlled, never with an Uncontrolled Beast.
